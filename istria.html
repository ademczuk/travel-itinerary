Your plan

Thursday 27 August

Vienna to Rovinj, straight through, no cave. 565 km, 6.3 hours of pure driving. Out of the house by 08:00, in Rovinj by about 16:00.

Dropping Postojna buys you an afternoon, and that is the real win. The cave was a 90 minute tour plus parking and the five kilometre detour, call it two and a half hours all in. Without it you are on a Rovinj beach the same day instead of arriving at half seven and going straight to dinner. On a four day trip that is a whole extra swim.

The drive, in order

Forecast that day: 29.6 C, 0 percent rain. The driest day of the window, so the afternoon you just freed up is a good one.

  1. 08:00, leave Vienna. A2 south toward Graz, then Spielfeld, Maribor, Ljubljana. This is the default road, not a clever detour: forcing the route through Maribor gives 564.9 km against 564.7 direct. Going via Villach and the Karawanken tunnel is 639.9 km, 75 km worse, and the Karawanken is a named late-August chokepoint with waits over an hour. You avoid it by doing nothing.
  2. About 200 km, charge at Ionity Kaiserwald, Raststation Kaiserwald, Premstaetten just south of Graz. Six stalls at 350 kW. Roughly 28 minutes, which is lunch.
  3. About 470 km, charge at Petrol Kozina, 350 kW, with a 50 kW and an AC point as backup. It sits on the road to the Dragonja crossing rather than off it, so it costs you nothing but the charging time.
  4. Border at Kastel-Dragonja map on the D510, joining the A9 at Umag. Schengen since 2023, so no passport or customs check, open around the clock.
  5. Rovinj by about 16:00. Check in, then straight down to the water. Vestar is ten minutes away and has showers, or Cuvi is seven minutes if you want somewhere quieter.
  6. Dinner at Giannino, two minutes from the old town, open daily in August, no booking needed. +385 52 813 402.

Friday 28 August

The pebble day. One beach, all day, ten minutes away. No alarm needed, this is the day you do not have to be anywhere.

Vestar, all day

Forecast: 30.0 C, 38 percent rain. Hottest and wettest of the window. plan for heat watch the sky

Vestar map is the one that actually answers what you asked for. Maistra, who run it, describe it as a "Pebble, rocky beach" with "natural shade". Blue Flag, showers, loungers and parasols for hire, a spray park for when they are bored of swimming, and a restaurant so lunch is not a logistics problem. A wide shallow bay, five kilometres south of Rovinj.

Why it suits a 30 degree day: the shade is natural and on the beach, so you are not driving anywhere at midday to escape the sun. Water shoes on both boys, the surface is pebble and rock and there are sea urchins on the rockier parts.

If it actually rains. Taking the caves out removed this trip's weather insurance, and it is worth saying so plainly rather than pretending otherwise. A wet Friday here is Rovinj old town, a long lunch, and the covered market. The indoor options are all still listed further down, marked as dropped rather than deleted, if you change your mind at breakfast.

Saturday 29 August

The big beach day, and there are two honest ways to do it. Decide at breakfast on what the sky is doing.

A. Rabac, the white pebble day

Forecast: 29.1 C, 36 percent rain. an hour each way

Rabac map is the honest answer to where the big pebble beaches are. Its own tourist board calls it the pearl of Kvarner Bay "due to its white pebble beaches". Girandella and Maslinica are the two named ones. Out by 09:00, and treat it as the whole day rather than something to fit around anything else.

Being straight with you: I could not find a published length for any Rabac beach, so "big" here rests on the tourist board's own description and on Rabac's reputation, not on a number I verified. If the drive sounds like too much for one swim, option B is the safer trade.

B. Stay local, two beaches in one day

Morning at Cuvi map, seven minutes south, a gravel and sand cove with pine shade and a gentle entry. It fills early, so go early and bring food. Then late afternoon in the Golden Cape map coves, which are five minutes from town and properly shaded by pine. No driving beyond ten minutes all day.

Or simply go back to Vestar. There is nothing wrong with doing the good beach twice, and with a five year old the second visit is usually the better one because nobody is working out where anything is.

If something goes wrong

Added after a review pointed out that this page told you where to go and not what to do when it fell apart.

Emergency 112, everywhere

112 is the emergency number in Slovenia and in Croatia and across the whole EU. The European Commission's own page: "112 is the European emergency phone number, available everywhere in the EU, free of charge", from landlines and mobiles, for ambulance, fire or police. It adds that operators in many countries answer in English or French as well as the national language, and that if you do not know where you are the operator can locate you. Save it in both phones before you go, because a five year old can dial three digits.

What to skip, and why

  • Cape Kamenjak. Beautiful, and the good coves need a long shadeless walk or a gravel track. Brutal at midday with a five year old.
  • Motovun on foot. Steep cobbled climb in full August sun with no shade until the top.
  • Lim Fjord and Pazin Abyss zipline courses. Height and age minimums rule out the five year old, and the standalone fjord cruise is 45 minutes of looking at a wall.
  • Anything stacked on the Brijuni day. The crossing plus the island tour is the day.

What I got wrong while planning this

Three corrections, because a page that only shows the polished answer teaches you nothing about how much to trust it.

One. I told you Postojna Cave was the charging stop, because it sits at 434 km of a 569 km drive and that is exactly where the second charge falls. It was a lovely coincidence and it was wrong. I inferred a charger from a convenient distance rather than checking, and the cave's own page says AC only, hotel guests only. Kozina, 24 minutes further, is the real stop.

Two. I flagged Aquarium Porec as temporarily closed on the strength of a search summary. The operator's own site says otherwise and so does the page the claim came from. A summary of a page is not the page.

Three, and the biggest. I told you Rovinj was full. It is not. Its hotels are full for a party of four, which is a much narrower claim, and I had measured one operator and generalised it to a whole town. Twenty-five apartments and villas came back available the moment I asked the question properly.
